## Background
<!-- type: background -->
Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD, encoded by DPYD) catabolizes most of an administered fluorouracil dose. Capecitabine is an oral prodrug of fluorouracil. Reduced DPD activity leads to fluorouracil accumulation and a risk of severe or fatal toxicity, including neutropenia, mucositis, diarrhea and neurotoxicity.

## Genotype to phenotype translation
<!-- type: translation -->
DPYD phenotype is assigned from a gene activity score, which is the sum of the activity values of the two alleles. A fully functional allele such as *1 has a value of 1. Decreased-function alleles such as c.2846A>T and c.1129-5923C>G (HapB3) have a value of 0.5. No-function alleles such as *2A (c.1905+1G>A) and *13 (c.1679T>G) have a value of 0.
- Activity score 2: Normal Metabolizer.
- Activity score 1 or 1.5: Intermediate Metabolizer.
- Activity score 0 or 0.5: Poor Metabolizer.

## Recommendation: Normal Metabolizer (fluorouracil)
<!-- type: recommendation; phenotype: Normal Metabolizer; drug: fluorouracil; action: standard -->
For DPYD normal metabolizers there is no indication to change the fluorouracil dose or therapy based on genotype. Use the label-recommended dose and administration.

## Recommendation: Intermediate Metabolizer (fluorouracil)
<!-- type: recommendation; phenotype: Intermediate Metabolizer; drug: fluorouracil; action: reduce_dose -->
For DPYD intermediate metabolizers, reduce the fluorouracil starting dose by 50 percent, then titrate the dose based on toxicity or therapeutic drug monitoring if available.

## Recommendation: Poor Metabolizer (fluorouracil)
<!-- type: recommendation; phenotype: Poor Metabolizer; drug: fluorouracil; action: avoid -->
For DPYD poor metabolizers, avoid fluorouracil. For an activity score of 0.5, if no suitable alternative agent exists, a strongly reduced dose with early therapeutic drug monitoring may be considered by a specialist. For an activity score of 0, do not use fluorouracil.

## Caveats
<!-- type: caveat -->
Genotyping covers only variants with established evidence, so the absence of these variants does not exclude DPD deficiency. Clinicians should still monitor for early signs of fluoropyrimidine toxicity in every patient.
